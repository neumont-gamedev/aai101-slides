The big question
Can human thought be replicated by logic and rules?

People have been asking this for over 2,000 years.

Aristotle
384–322 BC
Greek philosopher, founder of formal logic

Reasoning with rules

Aristotle tried to capture correct thinking as a set of rules. His key tool was the syllogism: two premises that force a conclusion.

PREMISE 1All humans are mortal.
PREMISE 2Socrates is a human.
THEREFORESocrates is mortal.
Notice: you could check this without knowing what “mortal” means. Reasoning by rules alone is something a machine can do. That idea is the seed of AI.

Claude Shannon
1916–2001
Mathematician and engineer, father of information theory

1937 · CLAUDE SHANNONFrom logic to circuits

Shannon showed that Boole’s algebra describes electrical switches: on = 1, off = 0.

  • Switches in a row (series) = AND
  • Switches side by side (parallel) = OR
Every digital computer, and every AI running on one, is built from Boole’s logic.

THE INSPIRATIONNeurons

Brain cells that talk using electrical and chemical signals.

  1. Dendrites receive signals
  2. The cell body adds them up
  3. If strong enough, it fires down the axon to other neurons

Billions of connected neurons give us thought, movement and perception.

Frank Rosenblatt
1928–1971
Psychologist and AI researcher, Cornell

1957 · FRANK ROSENBLATTThe Perceptron

The simplest neural network: one artificial neuron that makes a yes / no decision.

  • Each input (yes = 1, no = 0) is multiplied by a weight
  • Positive weights push toward GO, negative weights push toward STAY
  • If the total reaches the threshold, it fires: GO
  • Training = adjusting the weights until the answers are right
GO if Σ input × weight ≥ 0.75

1980 · RULES STRIKE BACKExpert systems

Programs that mimic a human expert’s decisions in one narrow field, such as diagnosing diseases or troubleshooting machines, using if-then rules.

01

Knowledge acquisition

Experts explain how they decide: their rules, facts and reasoning.

02

Knowledge base

That knowledge, stored as if-then rules. The system’s “brain.”

03

Inference engine

Applies the rules to new cases to reach conclusions, sometimes with a confidence level.

THE ALGORITHMMinimax

How a computer picks a move in two-player games like tic-tac-toe and chess: look ahead, and assume your opponent plays their best.

  1. Score the possible outcomes (the numbers at the bottom)
  2. Your opponent, MIN, will pick the lowest score for you
  3. You, MAX, pick the move whose worst case is the highest
Real games have millions of branches. The computer does exactly this, just much faster.

1986 · flashbackHow networks learn: backpropagation

  1. Forward: the network makes a guess.
  2. Loss: measure how wrong the guess was.
  3. Backward: send the error back through the layers to find how much each weight is to blame.
  4. Nudge: adjust every weight a tiny bit to reduce the error. Repeat thousands of times.

2016 · DeepMind (Google)AlphaGo beats the best at Go

  • Go: a 19 × 19 board and simple rules, but about 10170 possible positions. There are only about 1080 atoms in the universe.
  • Deep Blue-style search can’t cope. AlphaGo used neural networks to pick promising moves and judge who’s winning.
  • It studied human games, then got stronger by playing millions of games against itself.
  • Beat world champion Lee Sedol 4–1 in Seoul, March 2016. Its Move 37 in game 2 shocked the experts.
AlphaGo Zero (2017) skipped the human games entirely. Learning only from self-play, it beat the original AlphaGo 100–0.

Your turnPlay Go (9 × 9)

  1. Take turns placing a stone on an empty intersection. Black goes first.
  2. The empty points next to a group are its liberties. Take away all of them and the group is captured.
  3. You can’t play where your stones would have no liberties, unless that move captures. You also can’t repeat the board from the move before (ko).
  4. When both players pass, count your stones + the empty points you surround. White gets 6.5 bonus points (komi) for going second.

How AlphaGo improvedReinforcement learning

No labeled answers. An agent tries actions, gets a reward or a penalty, and slowly learns which moves pay off.

  • AlphaGo: win a game = +1, lose = −1. Millions of games against itself.
  • ChatGPT: people rated answers, and those ratings became the reward.

G
enerative

It creates new text by predicting the next word, again and again.

P
re-trained

It first reads a massive amount of text (books, articles, websites) to learn how language works. Later it can be fine-tuned for a job, like chatting.

T
ransformer

The network design from 2017. It uses self-attention to look at every word at once, so it can follow long sentences and train fast on GPUs.
