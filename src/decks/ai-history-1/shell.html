The big question
Can human thought be replicated by logic and rules?

People have been asking this for over 2,000 years.

Aristotle
384–322 BC
Greek philosopher, founder of formal logic

Reasoning with rules

Aristotle tried to capture correct thinking as a set of rules. His key tool was the syllogism: two premises that force a conclusion.

PREMISE 1All humans are mortal.
PREMISE 2Socrates is a human.
THEREFORESocrates is mortal.
Notice: you could check this without knowing what “mortal” means. Reasoning by rules alone is something a machine can do. That idea is the seed of AI.

DEFINITIONAutomaton

From the Greek αὐτόματον: “acting of one’s own will.”

A self-operating machine that follows a fixed sequence of operations or responds to preset instructions.

For centuries: mechanical puppets of people and animals, built to impress and entertain.

1726 · GULLIVER’S TRAVELSThe Lagado word machine

In Jonathan Swift’s satire, scholars at the Academy of Lagado use a machine to “write”:

  1. A wooden frame holds word tiles on wires
  2. Students turn cranks to shuffle the words
  3. Scholars copy down any phrase that seems to mean something as “knowledge”
Written as a joke, yet surprisingly close to a machine that generates ideas.

Ada Lovelace
1815–1852
Mathematician and writer, often called the first programmer

1843 · THE ANALYTICAL ENGINEThe first program

Charles Babbage designed the Analytical Engine, a general-purpose mechanical computer that was never fully built. Ada Lovelace wrote notes on it that included a step-by-step method for computing Bernoulli numbers, often called the first computer program.

“The Analytical Engine has no pretensions whatever to originate anything. It can do whatever we know how to order it to perform.”Ada Lovelace, 1843

Claude Shannon
1916–2001
Mathematician and engineer, father of information theory

1937 · CLAUDE SHANNONFrom logic to circuits

Shannon showed that Boole’s algebra describes electrical switches: on = 1, off = 0.

  • Switches in a row (series) = AND
  • Switches side by side (parallel) = OR
Every digital computer, and every AI running on one, is built from Boole’s logic.

THE INSPIRATIONNeurons

Brain cells that talk using electrical and chemical signals.

  1. Dendrites receive signals
  2. The cell body adds them up
  3. If strong enough, it fires down the axon to other neurons

Billions of connected neurons give us thought, movement and perception.

Frank Rosenblatt
1928–1971
Psychologist and AI researcher, Cornell

1957 · FRANK ROSENBLATTThe Perceptron

The simplest neural network: one artificial neuron that makes a yes / no decision.

  • Each input (yes = 1, no = 0) is multiplied by a weight
  • Positive weights push toward GO, negative weights push toward STAY
  • If the total reaches the threshold, it fires: GO
  • Training = adjusting the weights until the answers are right
GO if Σ input × weight ≥ 0.75

1980 · RULES STRIKE BACKExpert systems

Programs that mimic a human expert’s decisions in one narrow field, such as diagnosing diseases or troubleshooting machines, using if-then rules.

01

Knowledge acquisition

Experts explain how they decide: their rules, facts and reasoning.

02

Knowledge base

That knowledge, stored as if-then rules. The system’s “brain.”

03

Inference engine

Applies the rules to new cases to reach conclusions, sometimes with a confidence level.

DENDRAL

1960s · Stanford · chemistry
  • Identified the structure of organic molecules from mass-spectrometry data

THE ALGORITHMMinimax

Used in two-player games like chess and tic-tac-toe.

  1. Simulate every possible move and reply
  2. Score the end positions
  3. Assume both sides play their best: MAX picks the highest score, MIN the lowest
  4. Choose the move with the best guaranteed outcome

1997 · IBMDeep Blue

Defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov. Under the hood:

  • Minimax to weigh every move against every reply
  • Alpha-beta pruning to skip hopeless branches
  • A huge library of chess knowledge
  • Custom hardware evaluating millions of positions per second
