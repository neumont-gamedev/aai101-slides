Inside the networkFully connected

Every neuron in one layer connects to every neuron in the next. Each connection has its own weight, and each neuron adds a bias. Those numbers are what the network learns.

2012 · AlexNetThe deep learning moment

Before 2012, image recognition used hand-crafted features: people wrote code to find edges, corners and textures.

AlexNet (Krizhevsky, Sutskever & Hinton) was a deep convolutional neural network that learned its own features. It won with a top-5 error of 15.3%. Second place: 26.2%.

1986 · flashbackHow networks learn: backpropagation

  1. Forward: the network makes a guess.
  2. Loss: measure how wrong the guess was.
  3. Backward: send the error back through the layers to find how much each weight is to blame.
  4. Nudge: adjust every weight a tiny bit to reduce the error. Repeat thousands of times.

Goal: color the map so yellow dots land on yellow and blue on blue. The diagonal pattern is impossible for a single perceptron.

The computeThank video games

GPUs were built to shade millions of pixels at the same time for games. Training a neural network is the same kind of math: huge grids of multiply-and-add.

AlexNet was trained on two NVIDIA GTX 580 gaming cards in about five to six days.

A CPU has a few fast cores. A GPU has hundreds or thousands of simpler ones.

2016 · DeepMind (Google)AlphaGo beats the best at Go

  • Go: a 19 × 19 board and simple rules, but about 10170 possible positions. There are only about 1080 atoms in the universe.
  • Deep Blue-style search can’t cope. AlphaGo used neural networks to pick promising moves and judge who’s winning.
  • It studied human games, then got stronger by playing millions of games against itself.
  • Beat world champion Lee Sedol 4–1 in Seoul, March 2016. Its Move 37 in game 2 shocked the experts.
AlphaGo Zero (2017) skipped the human games entirely. Learning only from self-play, it beat the original AlphaGo 100–0.

How AlphaGo improvedReinforcement learning

No labeled answers. An agent tries actions, gets a reward or a penalty, and slowly learns which moves pay off.

  • AlphaGo: win a game = +1, lose = −1. Millions of games against itself.
  • ChatGPT: people rated answers, and those ratings became the reward.

Word embeddingsWords as points in space

Each token becomes a list of numbers: a position in “meaning space”. Words with similar meanings end up close together.

Directions mean something too: man → woman points the same way as king → queen.

2017 · GoogleAttention: every word looks at every other word

The core trickPredict the next word

A language model looks at the words so far and gives every possible next word a probability. Pick one, add it, repeat.

November 30, 2022ChatGPT goes public

  • Built on GPT-3.5, then trained with human feedback to follow instructions and hold a conversation.
  • Over 1 million users in its first 5 days.
  • Under the hood it’s still the same loop: predict the next token, add it, repeat.

Demo: pick a prompt and watch the answer appear one token at a time, with the runner-up choices underneath. (An illustration: real models choose from about 100,000 tokens.)

G
enerative

It creates new text by predicting the next word, again and again.

P
re-trained

It first reads a massive amount of text (books, articles, websites) to learn how language works. Later it can be fine-tuned for a job, like chatting.

T
ransformer

The network design from 2017. It uses self-attention to look at every word at once, so it can follow long sentences and train fast on GPUs.
